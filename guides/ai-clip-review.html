How to review AI clip suggestions without losing the story.

A few screenshots can help identify visual scenes, but they do not tell you what somebody said or what happened between frames. Here is a practical workflow for getting a useful clip.

Start with a goal, not a button

Decide who will watch the finished short and what they should understand at the end. For an interview, the unit of meaning is usually a complete answer. For a product demonstration, it may be a problem, a change, and a result. For gameplay, it may be an action that begins with a clear setup and ends with a visible result.

Select a narrow window first

In ClipForge Studio, load a video and scrub to a promising location. Set the IN and OUT fields around that sequence. The DeepSeek Vision beta examines up to twelve compressed still frames in a selected window of five to ninety seconds. Looking at samples is not the same as watching every frame. A critical moment may occur between samples, so the tool cannot guarantee that it finds the action you care about.

Review the suggestion in three passes

Pass one — visual relevance: does the recommended moment show the important person, object, or change? If not, ignore it. Pass two — context: move IN earlier if the viewer needs setup; move OUT later if you would otherwise cut before the conclusion. Pass three — rhythm: remove time that does not contribute to understanding while keeping the reaction or consequence.

Build a clear vertical composition

Choose 9:16 for a vertical short. Blur + fit preserves the entire horizontal video in the middle while filling unused space with a softened copy; Fill can crop content on the edges. Check whether faces, scoreboard text, hands, or products stay visible before choosing a mode.

Separate your title and lower text

Use the main title to tell the viewer what the clip is about. Use the lower text for context, a takeaway, or a call to action. Two editable text fields are more flexible than one headline moved from top to bottom. Keep lower text away from important information already present in the video, and preview it on the intended platform before posting.

Handle privacy carefully

ClipForge's basic editor processes your original video locally in the browser. If you enable AI, a limited number of sampled JPEG frames and your instructions leave your device via the ClipForge server to DeepSeek. Avoid analyzing confidential material, customers' private details, or footage you do not have permission to share. The optional private beta access code does not make a private video safe to transmit.

Export, inspect, and improve

Add only the useful segments to your sequence. Export a short test before committing to a long render, check the aspect ratio and audio, and compare the result against your original editing goal. AI is a suggestion tool, not a replacement for reviewing the final work.
