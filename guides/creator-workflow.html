Creator workflow · 7-minute guide

Create repeatable shorts without adding busywork

A useful tool saves time only when there is a clear editing process around it. Here is a practical workflow for freelancers, small brands, agencies, and creators repurposing their own long-form video.

6 min reading time

Start with a publishing goal

A client who wants viewers to understand a product has a different objective from a creator who wants people to subscribe to a podcast. Clarify what one short should accomplish before looking for footage.

Define the intended viewer, target platform, maximum clip length, and one useful takeaway. This prevents endless revisions after the clip is already cut.

Get the right source files

Ask for the highest-quality original that the client can share, plus permission to use it. Confirm whether the speaker, brand assets, logos and background music may appear in published clips.

For the current ClipForge version, one source file can be cut into multiple segments and joined. Combining different source files into one export is not supported yet.

Mark moments before editing

Watch at higher playback speed in your preferred video player if available and note timestamp ranges. Prioritize moments with a clear idea, example or reveal rather than just loud or fast action.

Create a rough list of five candidate clips for each long recording. Rank them for clarity, relevance to the target audience and the likelihood of working as standalone pieces.

Make an efficient first pass

Open the selected recording, set IN and OUT marks, and add the strongest segments in order. If a video needs 30 seconds, do not include 60 seconds of setup just because the source is interesting.

Choose 9:16 for vertical distribution or a wide frame when the content depends on slides, tables or product details. Fit + blur is a valid choice when cropping would erase information.

Use a short title, not fake subtitles

Write a headline that tells the viewer what they will get: “3 pricing mistakes for new shops” or “How this feature saves 20 minutes.” Use legible contrast and leave the focal subject visible.

Automatic speech captions, animated subtitle templates, and direct SNS publishing are not included in the current free Studio. Keep those as separate steps if a client requires them.

Control revisions and delivery

Name exports by source, topic, orientation and revision: interview-pricing-9x16-v1.webm, for example. Watch the final file, not only the preview, and check that the beginning and end are intact.

Browser exports may produce MP4 or WebM depending on the device. Before promising a delivery format, test the exact browser and computer the editor will use.

Measure time saved, not just output count

Record how many minutes it takes to find a candidate, complete a first edit and handle client changes. Then compare that total with the old workflow.

If the tool adds review time or frequent export failures, it is not actually saving money. The goal is a dependable production process, not a feature list.
