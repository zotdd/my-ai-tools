Interview editing · 7-minute guide

Turn one conversation into clear short clips

A focused spoken answer is often more valuable than an artificially dramatic jump cut. Use these steps to preserve meaning when repurposing interviews, podcasts, and long talks.

6 min reading time

Choose a self-contained answer

A strong short solves one question, explains one misconception, or shares one complete example. Listen for a beginning that defines the topic and an ending that finishes the thought. Do not grab only the strongest sentence if it changes the speaker’s meaning.

For a 40-minute conversation, start by writing down five timestamps where the guest finishes a useful idea. The shortlist is your rough cut; do not export everything.

Build a hook without changing context

A short usually needs a reason to keep watching in the first few seconds. A legitimate hook could be the interviewer’s question, a concise statement of the problem, or the guest’s precise answer.

If you open with an unusual quote, include the context that explains it. Reordering words to make a stronger claim creates misleading content and erodes viewer trust.

Set start and end points

Scrub the source video and find the moment the speaker starts their answer. Set IN just before the first meaningful word. Set OUT once the answer finishes and a brief natural pause follows.

If your short combines two parts of the same video, check whether the transition is logically and grammatically clear. The ClipForge Studio lets you choose multiple ranges from one source file and rearrange them.

Reframe for vertical viewing

Wide interview shots often include two speakers. A severe center crop can cut one of them off. Use the full-frame fit option with a blurred background when both faces must remain visible.

When there is one central speaker, fill and horizontal focus may work better. Check each part of the clip because a change in camera angle can move the face out of the crop.

Make text truthful and readable

Write one concise headline that gives context rather than inventing a claim. For example: “Why this founder stopped posting daily” explains the topic better than “THIS CHANGED EVERYTHING.”

This beta offers a single on-screen title overlay, not speech-to-text subtitles. If word-by-word captions are essential, plan an additional subtitle step in another editor.

Review before publishing

Play the entire export and check audio at segment boundaries, sentence continuity, spelling, and whether every face or on-screen graphic remains visible.

Only reuse conversations you own or have permission to republish. Editing tools do not create copyright permission.
