16:9 to 9:16: crop, fit or blur?

Choose the right vertical framing strategy for games, tutorials, live action and screen recordings.

6 min reading time

A vertical frame changes what the viewer can see

A horizontal 1920 × 1080 video has an aspect ratio of 16:9. A typical vertical short is 9:16. Keeping the same height while filling a vertical frame shows only a narrow slice of the original width. That's why a center crop may throw away the minimap, presenter or title you hoped viewers would see.

The choice isn't just aesthetic. You are deciding whether to prioritize large, immersive action or complete context.

Option 1: Fill

Fill enlarges the video until no bars remain, then crops the overflow. It works well when the important content stays near the center: a speaking face, a single golf swing, or an isolated action that doesn't depend on edge-of-screen UI. It works poorly when meaning is spread across the frame.

Use Fill when: a central subject is more important than the complete scene. Scrub through the whole clip rather than checking only the first frame; a player may move out of the crop during the payoff.

Option 2: Fit

Fit scales the entire image until it fits inside the vertical canvas, preserving every edge. The unused space is filled with a solid color. This is the clearest option for screen recordings, dense gameplay HUDs or slides where even small cropped details would be unacceptable.

Use Fit when: the content itself is information-dense and the background doesn't need visual decoration. Accept that the main image will appear smaller on a phone.

Option 3: Blur + fit

Blur + fit keeps the full horizontal image in front and uses a blurred enlargement of that image behind it. You preserve context while making the side or top/bottom margins feel intentional. It is a practical default for many gameplay shorts.

Use Blur + fit when: you want a polished vertical canvas without hiding the scoreboard or cutting important action. The blurred duplicate is a background, not additional information.

Zoom and focus: how much is too much?

Start at 100% zoom. Increase it only after checking the entire selected segment. A modest enlargement can make a small subject more legible, but it also removes more of the original frame. ClipForge's horizontal focus adjusts which part of the enlarged image remains visible. The focus slider has no effect if there's no horizontal overflow to move.

A quick decision table

SourceFirst tryWhy
MOBA or FPS full HUDBlur + fitKeeps controls, UI and map
Centered face or golf swingFillUses more phone-screen space
Software tutorialFitAvoids cutting menu labels
Wide group sceneBlur + fitKeeps all participants visible

Export and inspect on a phone

Previewing a vertical canvas on a desktop doesn't tell you whether small text will be readable at phone size. After export, open it on a phone if possible. Look for title contrast, thin UI text, and whether the viewer knows where to look. Reduce title length before increasing font size to the point it covers the action.
